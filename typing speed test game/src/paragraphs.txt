When Alexander the Great was still a boy, his tutor once told him, Every man is born with limits, even kings. Alexander stared at the horizon for a long time before replying, Then I will spend my life finding where mine end. Years later, standing before a conquered empire, his generals begged him to stop, to rest, to be content. But he looked over the endless lands still untouched by his banner and whispered, There are no limits, only men who accept them. He died young, not of defeat, but of the same fire that made him unstoppable. Some called it madness. The Stoics called it fate embraced by will.
